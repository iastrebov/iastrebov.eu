Every article on this blog is AI-supervised. That sentence is easy to read in the worst possible way: content farm, traffic bait, SEO text generated around keywords nobody cares about.

This is not what I am building.

The questions are mine. They come from subjects I wanted to understand, problems I met in engineering, issues I saw around me, and lessons I collected from experience. AI helps me wrap those thoughts into an article that another person can follow.

<!--more-->

## AI-Supervised Does Not Mean AI-Originated

There is important difference between the source of an idea and the tool used to present it.

The source here is my curiosity and experience. I decide which question deserves attention, what position I take, which details matter, and what should survive the final edit. AI can help organize rough notes, expose repetition, improve wording, and turn an unfinished explanation into a readable path.

It does not get to manufacture a career for me. It should not invent an incident, a benchmark, or certainty that I do not have. If a statement appears under my name, responsibility for it is also mine. “AI wrote it” is not an escape hatch.

## Why Use AI at All?

Because having something useful to say and presenting it clearly are different jobs.

An idea in my head may begin as fragments: one production concern, one argument from a code review, one pattern that keeps returning, or one technical question I want to settle. Raw experience is valuable, but raw notes are usually poor interface for a reader.

AI is part of that interface. It helps give the material a sequence, remove noise, and make the explanation easier to enter. Think about compiler: source still has an author, even when another system transforms it into a form people can run.

The transformation is useful. It is not authorship of the underlying experience.

## Searchable Is Not the Same as SEO Spam

I want articles to be discoverable. A clear title and description help a person find an answer. This becomes spam only when traffic is the purpose and the page has no real question behind it.

My direction is opposite. First comes something I care about. Then comes the article. Search presentation is packaging after that, not a factory choosing what I should pretend to care about.

Of course, not every post will be universal truth. Some are explanations, some are opinions, and some are records of how I currently see a problem. Experience has context. Good engineering judgment changes when constraints change, and my judgment can change when I learn more.

## The Human Part Must Remain Visible

AI has a dangerous talent: it can make weak thinking sound complete. Smooth paragraphs can hide a missing mechanism. Confident tone can hide uncertainty. Five tidy sections can create illusion that a messy problem is solved.

This is why supervision matters. The work is not finished when text becomes fluent. It is finished when the argument still represents what I mean, examples survive scrutiny, uncertainty is not erased, and the result is useful to somebody facing a similar question.

You may still disagree with me. Good. This blog should show a path, not issue commandments from machine-polished stone. Test the advice against your system, your team, and your constraints.

So this is the compact contract behind every post:

- the interests, questions, opinions, and experience are mine;
- AI helps structure, edit, and present them;
- I remain responsible for what gets published;
- usefulness matters more than traffic.

AI is in the workshop. It is not the reason the workshop exists.
